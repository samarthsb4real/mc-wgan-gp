3. DATASET AND PROBLEM FORMULATION
===================================

3.1 Magnetic Tile Surface Defect Dataset
-----------------------------------------
We use the Magnetic Tile surface defect dataset [Huang et al., 2020],
a publicly available benchmark for industrial surface inspection. The
dataset contains 1,344 grayscale images of magnetic tile surfaces across
6 classes, each paired with a binary segmentation mask indicating the
spatial location of defects.

Table 1 summarizes the dataset statistics:

  Class      | Samples | Train | Val  | Mask Coverage (mean) | Aspect Ratio Range
  -----------|---------|-------|------|----------------------|-------------------
  Blowhole   |   115   |   98  |  17  | 0.2% (0.0–0.7%)      | 0.7–2.7
  Break      |    85   |   72  |  13  | 4.3% (0.1–22.9%)     | 0.4–2.5
  Crack      |    57   |   48  |   9  | 0.5% (0.1–2.3%)      | 0.4–2.4
  Fray       |    32   |   27  |   5  | 17.1% (0.1–43.9%)    | 0.4–1.5
  Free       |   952   |  809  | 143  | 0.0% (all zero)      | 0.4–2.8
  Uneven     |   103   |   88  |  15  | 23.9% (0.0–48.5%)    | 0.4–2.5
  -----------|---------|-------|------|----------------------|-------------------
  Total      |  1,344  | 1,142 | 202  |                      |

The dataset exhibits severe class imbalance: the Free class (952 samples)
contains 35× more samples than the Fray class (27 samples). This imbalance
is representative of real industrial inspection scenarios where defect-free
surfaces dominate production output.

3.2 Data Characteristics
--------------------------
Images are variable-size grayscale photographs with heights ranging from
222 to 403 pixels and widths from 103 to 618 pixels. Aspect ratios vary
widely (0.4 to 2.8), reflecting the diverse orientations and sizes of
magnetic tile samples captured during inspection.

Segmentation masks are nominally binary but contain JPEG compression
artifacts (intermediate pixel values 0–255). We binarize masks at a
threshold of 127 during preprocessing. The Free class masks are all-zero
(no defect regions), while defect classes have varying coverage: Blowhole
defects are tiny (0.2% mean coverage), while Fray and Uneven defects
cover larger surface areas (17.1% and 23.9% respectively).

Dataset-level pixel statistics: mean = 0.431, std = 0.168 (normalized
to [0,1]). These statistics are consistent across classes, indicating
similar overall brightness and contrast.

3.3 Preprocessing
------------------
We apply the following preprocessing pipeline (implemented in preprocess.py):

1. Aspect-ratio-preserving padding: Each image is padded to square using
   the image mean intensity as fill value, then resized to 128×128 pixels
   using bilinear interpolation. This preserves spatial structure that
   would be distorted by direct square resize.

2. Mask preprocessing: Masks are padded with zeros (background) and
   resized using nearest-neighbor interpolation to preserve binary values,
   then binarized at threshold 127.

3. Free class masks: Correctly set to all-zeros (no defect region),
   rather than the all-ones fallback used in the original implementation.

4. Statistics computation: Dataset mean (0.431) and std (0.168) are
   computed from all preprocessed images and saved for normalization.

For GAN training, we use standard [-1,1] normalization (mean=0.5, std=0.5)
to match the Tanh output range of the generator. For classifier training,
we use the dataset statistics (mean=0.431, std=0.168) for better feature
learning.

3.4 Data Splits
----------------
We use stratified splitting to preserve class proportions in train and
validation sets. For the single-split experiments (Section 6.1), we use
an 85/15 train/validation split (1,142 train, 202 validation). For the
5-fold cross-validation experiments (Section 6.2), we use StratifiedKFold
with 5 folds and random_state=42, yielding approximately 1,075 training
and 269 validation samples per fold.

3.5 Problem Formulation
------------------------
Given a training set D = {(x_i, m_i, y_i)} where x_i is a grayscale image,
m_i is a binary segmentation mask, and y_i ∈ {0,...,5} is a class label,
our goal is to train a classifier f: X → Y that maximizes per-class F1
scores, particularly for minority classes (Break, Crack, Fray).

We augment D with synthetic samples G = {(x̂_j, y_j)} generated by a
mask-conditioned GAN trained on D, and evaluate whether D ∪ G yields
better classifier performance than D alone.
