1. INTRODUCTION
===============

Automated visual inspection of manufactured surfaces is a critical quality
control task in industrial production. Deep learning classifiers have achieved
strong performance on balanced benchmark datasets, but real-world inspection
data is inherently imbalanced: defect classes are rare by design, as
manufacturing processes are optimized to minimize defect rates. This creates
a fundamental challenge — the classes most important to detect correctly
(rare defects) are precisely those with the fewest training examples.

The Magnetic Tile surface defect dataset [Huang et al., 2020] exemplifies
this challenge. It contains 1,344 grayscale images across 6 classes, with
class counts ranging from 27 (Fray) to 952 (Free/defect-free). Standard
ResNet-18 classifiers trained on this data achieve high overall accuracy
(~94–98%) but this metric is dominated by the majority Free class, which
constitutes 71% of the dataset. Minority class F1 scores — the metrics that
matter for defect detection — are substantially lower and highly variable.

Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) offer a principled approach to
synthetic data augmentation: train a generator to produce realistic defect
images conditioned on class labels and spatial masks, then augment the
training set with synthetic minority class samples. Mask-conditioned GANs
are particularly appealing for defect synthesis because segmentation masks
encode the spatial geometry of defects — where on the surface the defect
appears — providing a structural prior that should guide generation of
spatially coherent defect patterns.

However, conditional GANs are susceptible to a failure mode we term
"conditioning bypass": the generator learns to ignore the conditioning
signal and produces outputs that are independent of the provided condition.
This failure is particularly insidious because the generator may still
produce visually plausible images (passing qualitative inspection) while
completely failing to respect class-specific conditioning. The result is
synthetic images that are indistinguishable across classes — useless or
harmful for augmentation.

In this paper, we make the following contributions:

1. We identify and formally characterize a conditioning bypass bug in a
   Mask-Conditioned WGAN-GP architecture, demonstrating the failure through
   cross-class SSIM analysis and minority class distinctiveness testing.

2. We propose and implement a multi-pronged architectural fix comprising:
   (a) multi-point mask injection at 5 locations in the generator decoder,
   (b) 2-channel critic input (image + mask) for gradient pressure,
   (c) auxiliary segmentation head with mask reconstruction loss.

3. We conduct a rigorous evaluation using 5-fold stratified cross-validation
   with McNemar's statistical significance testing, comparing GAN augmentation
   against a properly controlled oversampling ablation baseline.

4. We provide an honest analysis of the negative result: GAN augmentation
   does not significantly improve over baseline on this severely imbalanced
   small dataset, while oversampling does. We analyze the mechanistic reasons
   and provide recommendations for practitioners.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 reviews
related work on GAN-based augmentation and industrial defect detection.
Section 3 describes the dataset and problem formulation. Section 4 presents
the MC-WGAN-GP architecture and the conditioning bypass fix. Section 5
describes the experimental setup. Section 6 presents results and analysis.
Section 7 discusses limitations and future work. Section 8 concludes.
