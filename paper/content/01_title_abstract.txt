TITLE
=====
Conditioning Bypass in Mask-Conditioned GANs: Diagnosis, Fix, and Evaluation
on Industrial Surface Defect Data

ABSTRACT
========
Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) have shown promise for augmenting
imbalanced industrial inspection datasets, where minority defect classes are
severely underrepresented. However, conditioning mechanisms in GANs are
susceptible to bypass failures — where the generator learns to ignore
conditioning signals and collapses to a single output mode regardless of
the provided class label or spatial mask. In this work, we identify, diagnose,
and fix a conditioning bypass bug in a Mask-Conditioned Wasserstein GAN with
Gradient Penalty (MC-WGAN-GP) trained on the Magnetic Tile surface defect
dataset (1,344 images, 6 classes, severe imbalance: 27–952 samples per class).

The original architecture suffered from three compounding failure modes:
(1) mask signal washout due to single-point injection, (2) critic blindness
to spatial masks providing no gradient pressure for mask-consistent generation,
and (3) absence of auxiliary mask reconstruction accountability. We address
these through a multi-point mask injection scheme (5 injection points across
the generator decoder), a 2-channel critic input (image + mask), and an
auxiliary segmentation head trained with binary cross-entropy loss.

We evaluate the fixed architecture using 5-fold stratified cross-validation
with three conditions: baseline (real data only), GAN augmentation (real +
200 synthetic images per minority class), and oversampling ablation (real
data duplicated to equivalent counts). McNemar's test confirms that GAN
augmentation does not significantly differ from baseline (p=0.832), while
oversampling significantly outperforms both baseline (p=0.0005) and GAN
augmentation (p=0.0002). Per-class FID scores of 100–118 (vs. ~215 for the
untrained generator) confirm the fixed generator learned meaningful structure.
Synthetic image variance ratios of 0.82–0.92 relative to real images confirm
non-trivial signal content.

Our results suggest that for severely imbalanced datasets with fewer than
100 minority class samples, GAN augmentation does not provide statistically
significant improvement over simple oversampling, and may introduce
distributional mismatch that degrades classifier performance. We provide
a complete analysis of failure modes, training dynamics, and recommendations
for practitioners.
