6. RESULTS AND ANALYSIS
========================

6.1 GAN Training Dynamics
---------------------------
Figure 2 shows the training dynamics over 100 epochs. The Wasserstein
distance peaked at epoch 26 (W=53.97) and declined thereafter, indicating
the critic eventually dominated. The critic loss stabilized around -30 to
-40 from epoch 20 onward. Generator loss increased monotonically, which
is expected behavior in WGAN-GP as the critic strengthens.

The epoch 26 checkpoint was selected as the best generator based on peak
Wasserstein distance, corresponding to the point of maximum generator
competitiveness before critic memorization dominates.

6.2 Synthetic Image Quality
-----------------------------
Per-class FID scores from the epoch 26 checkpoint:
  Break: 100.32  |  Crack: 117.94  |  Fray: 112.30

All three minority classes passed the quality gate (threshold=300).
The untrained generator baseline FID of ~215 confirms the trained
generator achieved a 2× improvement in distributional similarity.

Synthetic image variance ratios (synth/real): 0.924 (Break), 0.826 (Crack),
0.820 (Fray). These ratios confirm the synthetic images carry real signal
rather than noise or blank outputs.

6.3 5-Fold Cross-Validation Results
--------------------------------------
Table 4 presents the main results across all three conditions:

  Condition        | Accuracy (%)      | Macro F1 | Break F1 (%)      | Crack F1 (%)      | Fray F1 (%)
  -----------------|-------------------|----------|-------------------|-------------------|------------------
  Baseline         | 94.64 ± 1.92      | 0.9033   | 85.13 ± 9.02      | 91.54 ± 6.27      | 80.63 ± 7.92
  GAN Augmented    | 94.42 ± 0.52      | 0.8947   | 86.72 ± 1.38      | 84.77 ± 6.50      | 81.23 ± 9.80
  Oversampling     | 96.65 ± 0.41      | 0.9295   | 91.12 ± 3.43      | 92.58 ± 3.99      | 83.03 ± 6.76

Key observations:

(1) Oversampling significantly outperforms both baseline and GAN augmented
    conditions in overall accuracy (+2.01% and +2.23% respectively) and
    macro F1 (+0.026 and +0.035 respectively).

(2) GAN augmentation does not significantly improve over baseline in
    overall accuracy (-0.22%) or macro F1 (-0.009).

(3) GAN augmentation reduces variance substantially: std drops from 1.92%
    (baseline) to 0.52% (GAN augmented). This variance reduction is
    consistent with the synthetic images providing a regularization effect,
    but the mean performance does not improve.

(4) Crack F1 decreases with GAN augmentation (91.54% → 84.77%), suggesting
    the synthetic Crack images introduce distributional mismatch. Crack has
    the smallest mask coverage (0.5% mean), making it the hardest class
    for the generator to condition on correctly.

(5) Break F1 shows a modest improvement with GAN augmentation
    (85.13% → 86.72%), though this is within the noise margin.

6.4 McNemar's Test Results
----------------------------
Table 5 presents the McNemar's test results on full 5-fold CV predictions
(n=1,344):

  Comparison                      | χ²      | p-value | Significant
  --------------------------------|---------|---------|------------
  Baseline vs. GAN Augmented      | 0.0449  | 0.8321  | No
  Baseline vs. Oversampling       | 12.291  | 0.0005  | Yes ***
  GAN Augmented vs. Oversampling  | 13.565  | 0.0002  | Yes ***

The McNemar's test confirms:
- GAN augmentation does NOT significantly differ from baseline (p=0.832).
  The observed accuracy difference is consistent with sampling noise.
- Oversampling IS significantly better than both baseline (p=0.0005)
  and GAN augmentation (p=0.0002).

This is the central finding of the paper: on this severely imbalanced
small dataset, simple oversampling with real image duplication significantly
outperforms GAN-based synthetic augmentation.

6.5 Analysis of GAN Augmentation Failure
------------------------------------------
We identify three mechanistic explanations for why GAN augmentation
fails to improve over baseline:

(1) Distributional Mismatch: The t-SNE visualization of ResNet-18 features
    shows that synthetic images for Crack and Fray classes occupy different
    regions of feature space compared to real images of the same class.
    The centroid distance between real and synthetic Crack features is
    substantially larger than for Break, consistent with the Crack F1
    degradation observed with GAN augmentation.

(2) Insufficient Training Data for Generator: With only 27–85 real images
    per minority class, the generator cannot learn the full diversity of
    defect appearances. The generator tends to produce a limited set of
    defect patterns, reducing the effective diversity of the augmented
    training set.

(3) Mask Coverage Mismatch: Crack defects have extremely small mask
    coverage (0.5% mean), making it difficult for the generator to learn
    spatially precise defect placement. The auxiliary segmentation head
    loss (λ_mask=2.0) may be insufficient to enforce precise mask
    adherence for such small defect regions.

(4) Confound: Adding 600 synthetic images increases training set size by
    52%. The oversampling ablation controls for this by duplicating real
    images to the same count. The fact that oversampling significantly
    outperforms GAN augmentation despite using the same training set size
    suggests the synthetic images are of lower quality than real images
    for classifier training purposes.

6.6 Comparison with Single-Split Results
------------------------------------------
For reference, single-split results (85/15 train/val, no cross-validation):
  Baseline:      95.54% val accuracy (epoch 22)
  GAN Augmented: 93.56% val accuracy (epoch 20)

These results are consistent with the 5-fold CV findings but have higher
variance due to the small validation set (202 samples). The 5-fold CV
results are more reliable and should be used for all comparisons.
