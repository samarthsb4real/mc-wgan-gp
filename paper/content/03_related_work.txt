2. RELATED WORK
===============

2.1 GAN-Based Data Augmentation
--------------------------------
Since the introduction of GANs by Goodfellow et al. [2014], numerous works
have explored their use for data augmentation in classification tasks.
Shrivastava et al. [2017] demonstrated that synthetic images from GANs can
improve classifier performance when real data is scarce. Frid-Adar et al.
[2018] applied GANs to augment liver lesion datasets, showing improvements
in CNN classification accuracy. However, these works typically operate on
datasets with hundreds to thousands of samples per class — a regime
substantially different from the severely imbalanced industrial inspection
setting we consider.

More recent work has highlighted the limitations of GAN augmentation.
Bowles et al. [2018] found that GAN augmentation can hurt performance when
the generator produces out-of-distribution samples. Tanaka et al. [2019]
showed that oversampling with simple duplication often matches or outperforms
GAN augmentation on small datasets, a finding consistent with our results.

2.2 Conditional and Mask-Conditioned GANs
------------------------------------------
Conditional GANs (cGANs) [Mirza and Osindero, 2014] extend the GAN framework
by conditioning both generator and discriminator on auxiliary information
such as class labels. Pix2Pix [Isola et al., 2017] demonstrated image-to-image
translation conditioned on spatial maps, establishing the paradigm of
mask-conditioned generation. SPADE [Park et al., 2019] introduced spatially
adaptive normalization for semantic image synthesis, showing that spatial
conditioning at multiple decoder layers prevents signal washout — a key
insight that motivates our multi-point injection approach.

The conditioning bypass failure we identify is related to the "posterior
collapse" phenomenon in conditional VAEs [Lucas et al., 2019] and the
"mode dropping" failure in GANs [Srivastava et al., 2017]. However, to our
knowledge, the specific failure mode of spatial mask conditioning bypass —
where the generator produces identical outputs regardless of mask geometry —
has not been formally characterized in the industrial inspection literature.

2.3 Industrial Defect Detection and Synthesis
----------------------------------------------
Industrial surface defect detection has been studied extensively using
classical computer vision [Xie, 2008] and deep learning approaches
[Weimer et al., 2016]. The Magnetic Tile dataset [Huang et al., 2020]
has become a standard benchmark, with reported classification accuracies
of 95–99% using ResNet and EfficientNet architectures.

GAN-based synthesis for industrial defects has been explored by several
groups. Niu et al. [2020] applied DCGAN to generate steel surface defects,
reporting improved classifier performance. Dong et al. [2019] used
conditional GANs for fabric defect synthesis. However, these works
typically do not include rigorous statistical significance testing or
controlled ablation studies comparing GAN augmentation to oversampling,
making it difficult to assess whether the reported improvements are due
to synthetic data quality or simply increased training set size.

2.4 WGAN-GP and Training Stability
------------------------------------
The Wasserstein GAN [Arjovsky et al., 2017] addresses training instability
in standard GANs by replacing the Jensen-Shannon divergence with the
Wasserstein-1 distance, providing more meaningful gradients throughout
training. The gradient penalty variant [Gulrajani et al., 2017] enforces
the Lipschitz constraint through a differentiable penalty term, enabling
stable training without weight clipping. We adopt WGAN-GP as our base
framework due to its established stability properties.

The Two Time-Scale Update Rule (TTUR) [Heusel et al., 2017] proposes
using different learning rates for generator and discriminator to achieve
local Nash equilibria. We adopt TTUR with a 2:1 critic-to-generator
learning rate ratio, reduced from the commonly used 4:1 ratio to prevent
critic dominance on our small dataset.

2.5 Evaluation of Synthetic Data Quality
------------------------------------------
The Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) [Heusel et al., 2017] measures the
distance between real and synthetic image distributions in InceptionV3
feature space. FID has become the standard metric for GAN evaluation,
with scores below 50 generally considered high quality for natural images.
For industrial grayscale images with constrained textures and small sample
sizes, FID computation requires careful handling of degenerate covariance
matrices — we address this through PCA dimensionality reduction before
FID computation.

McNemar's test [McNemar, 1947] is the appropriate statistical test for
comparing two classifiers on the same test set, as it accounts for the
paired nature of predictions. Despite its importance, McNemar's test is
rarely applied in GAN augmentation papers, making it difficult to assess
whether reported improvements are statistically significant.
