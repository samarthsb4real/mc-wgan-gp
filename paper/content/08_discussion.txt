7. DISCUSSION
==============

7.1 Interpretation of the Negative Result
-------------------------------------------
The central finding — that GAN augmentation does not significantly improve
over baseline, while oversampling does — is a negative result that deserves
careful interpretation rather than dismissal.

The negative result is not a failure of the GAN architecture. The fixed
MC-WGAN-GP produces synthetic images with FID scores of 100–118 (vs. ~215
for the untrained generator), variance ratios of 0.82–0.92 relative to
real images, and visually plausible defect patterns. The generator learned
meaningful structure. The failure is not in image quality per se, but in
the utility of that quality for classifier training.

The key insight is that distributional similarity (as measured by FID) is
a necessary but not sufficient condition for augmentation utility. Synthetic
images can be visually plausible and have reasonable FID scores while still
introducing distributional mismatch in the feature space that a classifier
uses for discrimination. The t-SNE analysis confirms this: synthetic images
cluster differently from real images in ResNet-18 feature space, particularly
for Crack — the class with the smallest defect regions and most challenging
conditioning signal.

7.2 When Does GAN Augmentation Help?
--------------------------------------
Our results suggest GAN augmentation is most likely to help when:

(1) Sufficient training data exists for the generator to learn diverse
    defect appearances (typically >200 samples per class).

(2) Defect regions are large enough for the generator to learn precise
    spatial conditioning (mask coverage >5%).

(3) The generator can be validated to produce class-discriminative outputs
    (SSIM < 0.7 across classes, classifier accuracy > 90% on synthetic data).

For the Magnetic Tile dataset, none of these conditions are fully met for
the most challenging minority classes (Fray: 27 samples, 17.1% coverage;
Crack: 57 samples, 0.5% coverage). Oversampling, which preserves the exact
real image distribution, is a more reliable augmentation strategy in this
regime.

7.3 The Conditioning Bypass Fix
---------------------------------
While the augmentation result is negative, the architectural fix for the
conditioning bypass bug is a genuine contribution. The original generator
produced SSIM > 0.95 across all class pairs — effectively a single-mode
generator. The fixed generator produces class-differentiated outputs with
FID scores that are 2× better than the untrained baseline.

The multi-point mask injection approach (5 injection points) is directly
motivated by the SPADE architecture [Park et al., 2019], which demonstrated
that spatially adaptive normalization at multiple decoder layers prevents
conditioning signal washout. Our implementation adapts this principle to
the WGAN-GP framework with explicit mask encoding concatenation rather than
normalization modulation.

The auxiliary segmentation head provides a direct gradient signal for mask
accountability. Without it, the generator can achieve low adversarial loss
while completely ignoring mask geometry. The BCE loss (λ_mask=2.0) provides
a regularization term that enforces spatial consistency without dominating
the adversarial signal.

7.4 Training Dynamics and Checkpoint Selection
------------------------------------------------
The W distance trajectory (peak at epoch 26, decline thereafter) reflects
a known failure mode for WGAN-GP on small datasets: the critic eventually
memorizes the real distribution rather than learning a smooth Lipschitz
function. This causes the gradient signal to the generator to degrade,
leading to the observed W distance decline.

The epoch 26 checkpoint selection is theoretically justified: it captures
the generator at the point of maximum competitiveness before critic
memorization dominates. In practice, this corresponds to the generator
that produces the most diverse and realistic outputs, as confirmed by
the FID scores.

Future work could address this through: (1) spectral normalization in the
critic to prevent memorization, (2) data augmentation during GAN training
(differentiable augmentation [Zhao et al., 2020]), or (3) transfer learning
from a GAN pre-trained on a larger industrial dataset.

7.5 Limitations
----------------
(1) Validation set size: With 5–15 minority class samples per fold in
    5-fold CV, per-class F1 estimates have high variance (±0.05–0.20).
    The McNemar's test on overall accuracy is reliable (n=1,344), but
    per-class conclusions should be interpreted cautiously.

(2) Single dataset: Our conclusions are drawn from a single dataset.
    The generalizability of the negative result to other industrial
    inspection datasets with different characteristics (larger minority
    classes, different defect types) is unknown.

(3) GAN hyperparameter sensitivity: We did not perform extensive
    hyperparameter search for the GAN. Different λ_mask values, learning
    rates, or architecture choices might yield better synthetic image
    quality and different augmentation outcomes.

(4) Fixed synthetic set: In our 5-fold CV, the synthetic images are
    generated from the full dataset and used across all folds. A more
    rigorous protocol would generate fold-specific synthetic images from
    each fold's training data, but this would require 5× the GAN training
    time.

(5) FID calibration: FID scores of 100–118 are difficult to interpret
    in absolute terms for grayscale industrial images. The natural image
    FID benchmarks (FID < 50 = good quality) may not apply to this domain.
    Domain-specific FID calibration would strengthen the quality assessment.
